### Lesson 1: Alpha Control: What It Is and Why It Replaces a Wall of Controllers

Most smart building installs end up with a patchwork of single-purpose controllers: one box for lighting, another for curtains, another for HVAC zoning, another for the sound system — each with its own wiring, its own app, and its own point of failure. Alpha Control is Finix Systems' answer to that patchwork: a single stackable control hub built to run a whole floor, a whole production zone, or an entire small factory from one board.

At its core (the Alpha Core module), it exposes 16 AC input channels and 16 relay output channels — enough to sense 16 separate switches/sensors and independently switch 16 separate loads from one unit. That single board can cover lighting circuits, motorized curtains and shutters, smart plugs, sound routing, and security/access integration simultaneously, which is what lets it functionally replace a whole cabinet of dedicated single-purpose controllers in a typical installation.

**Why this matters to an installer, practically:** fewer boxes to mount means faster installs and a cleaner panel; fewer separate devices talking to the network means fewer possible points of failure and fewer firmware versions to keep track of; and because the whole unit runs one shared firmware across the core and all connected expansion boards, a single OTA update keeps everything in sync rather than updating five separate gadgets individually.

**Core specs to remember for quoting a job:** input voltage range 90–265V AC (works across most regional mains standards without a separate converter), relay rating 10A @ 250V AC per channel for standard loads, powered itself via a 12V DC / 3A barrel-jack supply, and standby power draw under 2W — low enough that leaving it always-on has negligible impact on the client's electricity bill.

### Lesson 2: Protocols & Connectivity: Why Alpha Control Isn't Locked to One Platform

One of the most important things to communicate to a client evaluating Alpha Control is that it was designed protocol-agnostic from the start — it doesn't force them into one ecosystem. It speaks Wi-Fi (2.4GHz) and Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE 4.2/5.0) for consumer-grade wireless connections, a wired Ethernet 10/100 RJ45 port for a rock-solid always-on connection, RS-485 with Modbus RTU master/slave support for industrial daisy-chaining and integration with existing industrial equipment, and I2C/SPI buses for connecting additional sensors directly at the board level. On top of that physical layer, it runs MQTT with TLS support for secure IoT messaging, plus HTTP, WebSocket, and Modbus TCP for software integration.

**What that protocol list translates to for platform compatibility:** the unit works natively with eWeLink (the same app ecosystem used across other Finix/SONOFF-family products, useful for clients who already have devices in that app), Home Assistant (for technically engaged clients wanting full local control and deep customization), and Finix Cloud (the manufacturer's own dashboard, aimed at simpler management and centralized multi-unit oversight). Voice control reaches Alexa and Google Assistant indirectly, routed through the eWeLink integration rather than a direct proprietary skill.

**The practical installer takeaway:** because Alpha Control supports full local LAN/RS-485 control in addition to cloud OTA updates, it keeps working for on-site control even if the client's internet connection drops — a meaningful reliability advantage over pure cloud-dependent devices, and a genuine selling point when quoting an industrial job where downtime has a real cost.

**Daisy-chaining and scale:** the unit is explicitly built to scale linearly — you can daisy-chain multiple Alpha Control units over RS-485 or Ethernet and group dozens of them under one shared Home Assistant or Finix Cloud dashboard for centralized management, which is the intended approach for anything larger than a single-zone install (e.g. a multi-floor building or a full production line).

### Lesson 3: The Three Expansion Modules & When to Use Each

Alpha Control's stackable design is its most distinctive feature: the Alpha Core provides 3 expansion child-board slots, and each child board mounts directly onto the core via dedicated headers — no extra wiring runs required — with the core auto-detecting whatever's plugged in and exposing those channels through every supported platform automatically. As an installer, choosing the right combination of child boards for a job is a core part of the design conversation with a client, not an afterthought.

**High-Current Module** — 4 channels at 50A each, 200A total capacity for this module (and the brochure cites 300A+ total system capacity when combined with the core's own switching). Use case: loads far beyond what a standard 10A relay channel can safely handle — EV chargers, industrial motors, heat pumps, welding stations, large pumps and compressors. If a job spec sheet mentions any of those loads, this module isn't optional, it's mandatory — attempting to run them through the core's standard 10A channels would be a serious safety violation.

**Sound System Module** — 8 channels of multi-zone audio routing with volume and source control. Use case: multi-room audio, ceiling speaker zoning, paging systems, and ambient background music for retail or hospitality venues. This is the module to spec whenever a project brief mentions whole-home audio or a commercial space wanting zoned background music.

**Interlock Module** — 4 channels of hardware interlock logic with fail-safe operation. Use case: anywhere two loads must never be energized simultaneously for safety reasons — door access control circuits, motor reversing pairs (forward/reverse can't both engage at once), and any mutually exclusive load group. The key word here is *hardware* interlock — this protection is enforced at the circuit level, not just in software/firmware logic, so it keeps working correctly even during a firmware fault or network outage, which is exactly the guarantee safety-critical circuits need.

**Choosing modules for a real job — a worked scenario:** a small manufacturing floor needs 3 conveyor motors (each drawing well above 10A — High-Current Module), a paging system across 4 zones (Sound System Module), and a safety interlock so the conveyor and the loading-dock door motor can never run at the same time (Interlock Module). That's exactly 3 modules — the maximum the core supports — covering all three functional needs from a single Alpha Core unit, with no additional standalone controllers required.

### Lesson 4: Safety, Environmental Ratings & Installation Considerations

Alpha Control is built for both residential and industrial environments, and its protection features reflect that dual purpose — an installer should be able to explain each of them to a client, not just recite the spec sheet.

**Built-in protection layers:**
- **Opto-isolated inputs** — the 16 AC input channels are electrically isolated from the control logic using optical coupling, meaning a voltage spike or fault on the sensed circuit can't physically reach and damage the core's processing electronics.
- **Fused outputs** — the relay outputs are individually protected by fuses, so a fault on one output channel blows that channel's fuse rather than taking down the whole board or starting a fire risk.
- **Surge and TVS protection** — Transient Voltage Suppression components absorb brief high-voltage spikes (from lightning-induced surges or motor switching nearby) before they can reach sensitive components.
- **Hardware interlock (via the Interlock Module)** — as covered in the modules lesson, this safety layer is enforced in circuitry, not software, so it survives firmware crashes.

**Environmental and mechanical ratings to check against a job site before quoting:**
- Operating temperature range: **-10°C to +55°C** — check this against any planned outdoor, rooftop, or unconditioned mechanical-room installation location; an enclosure with some passive ventilation or shade may be needed at the upper end of that range in hot climates.
- Humidity tolerance: **10–90% non-condensing** — the "non-condensing" qualifier matters: this unit is not rated for locations where condensation regularly forms (e.g. unheated spaces with large temperature swings), and an enclosure with ventilation or a drip loop should be planned for borderline sites.
- Mounting: **DIN rail or panel mount** — standard electrical-panel mounting, meaning it drops into a typical distribution board or control cabinet without custom brackets.
- Certification: **CE and RoHS certified**, with FCC certification listed as pending at the time of this datasheet — worth double-checking current certification status before quoting a project in a market where FCC compliance is a hard requirement.

**A pre-installation checklist I'd give a technician for any Alpha Control job:** confirm the site's mains voltage falls within 90–265V AC before committing to skip a separate voltage converter; confirm total connected relay load per channel doesn't exceed 10A (route anything higher to the High-Current Module instead); confirm the mounting location's ambient temperature and humidity fall within the rated ranges, especially for outdoor or mechanical-room placements; and confirm which of the 3 expansion slots will be populated up front, since retrofitting a module later means reopening the panel.
